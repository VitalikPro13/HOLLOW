# Accepted risks

What we knowingly do not defend against, why, and when we look again. An
entry here is a decision, not an oversight: each has an owner and a reason
that survives a professional auditor reading it.

| AR-01 | The relay has no per-message rate limits, although ASVS 17.3.1 wants signalling to survive floods and RFC 9420 16.8 says KeyPackage requests SHOULD be rate-limited. | Revisited in 0.13 (2026-10-23, phase G2): still no per-message limit; a circuit breaker counts work in shadow mode and a send budget bounds the relay's held bytes. Was accepted (2026-09-26) | Message and binary rate limits were tried and broke the core: a reconnect burst (sync requests, channel probes, profiles, signalling) exceeds any limit, and dropped frames wedge CRDT sync. The protections that stay: per-IP connection caps (34 per address, 10 new per minute while the address holds a socket and 30 while it holds none, IPv6 /64), guest mode with its own limits and room cap, CAKE per-host fair share on the official relay's NIC (a setting on that box, not part of the repo's units or the setup wizard, so a self-hosted relay has it only if its operator adds it), coturn locked to the relay's own peers, the 64 MB payload ceiling and hard backpressure. G2 (0.13): every frame is charged in work units (frames, the writes they cause, signature checks, door proofs, audience size) against session buckets sized above a member of ten 1,536-device servers, and a frame the bucket cannot pay for closes the socket with code 1013 and a reconnect hint (`breaker.h`). A session's frame is never dropped: it stays uncounted and is resent on resume. A fetch or guest socket, which has no session, loses that frame and pauses 30 s. It ships in shadow mode: the relay counts what it would trip and closes nothing, `auth_ok` names the credit, and `enforced` is set only on an enforcing relay, so 0.13 clients pace themselves the same way (`relay_pace.rs`) without waiting on a shadow relay. The device ladder steps only when a session overspends the whole honest budget within 10 minutes (cap plus 10 minutes of refill, 1.4M units); any other trip is a pause. Separately, the relay's held bytes are bounded (`send_budget.h`): at most 64 MB per socket and a global budget of a quarter of RAM by default, never below 512 MiB or a third of a smaller box's RAM, which an operator may change but not push below that floor, charged per address share; under pressure the least progressed reader of the heaviest share is closed first. That is memory safety, not a volume quota. The breaker's numbers are compiled in; an operator cannot lower them. | Vitalii | 0.14: switch the breaker to enforce once its shadow counters show no honest trips, after plan-level tests for junk lock proofs and non-member leaves (relay mutations C6 and C9 survived). |

| AR-06 | Every `FileRequest` re-encrypts and re-streams the whole file, so a peer entitled to a file can make us upload it repeatedly (candidate H21). | Accepted (2026-09-27) | Honest clients ask only when they need the bytes (a download, a decrypt-fail retry).
