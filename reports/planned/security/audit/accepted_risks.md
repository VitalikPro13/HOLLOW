# Accepted risks

What we knowingly do not defend against, why, and when we look again. An
entry here is a decision, not an oversight: each has an owner and a reason
that survives a professional auditor reading it.

| ID | Risk | Decision | Reason | Owner | Review |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AR-01 | The relay has no per-message rate limits, although ASVS 17.3.1 wants signalling to survive floods and RFC 9420 16.8 says KeyPackage requests SHOULD be rate-limited. | Revisited in 0.14 (2026-11-12, track t14-relay): still no per-message limit; the circuit breaker enforces by default and the connection limits are operator settings. Revisited in 0.13 (2026-10-23, phase G2): a circuit breaker counting work in shadow mode and a send budget bounding the relay's held bytes. Was accepted (2026-09-26) | Message and binary rate limits were tried and broke the core: a reconnect burst (sync requests, channel probes, profiles, signalling) exceeds any limit, and dropped frames wedge CRDT sync. The protections that stay: per-address connection limits the operator sets (`SELF_HOSTING.md` "Connection limits"; by default 512 open and 120 new a minute after a first burst of 512 per IPv4 address, 128 open and 30 a minute after a burst of 128 per IPv6 /64, at most 256 logins in progress per IPv4 /24 or IPv6 /48 and 10,000 on the whole relay, and a budget of connected sockets set from memory; every limit refusal closes 1008, and a value out of range counts as the nearer end), guest mode with its own limits and room cap, CAKE per-host fair share on the relay's uplink: since 0.14 the setup wizard puts it on every relay it sets up (`hollow-cake.service`, on by default, sized under the line's measured or reported speed; an operator may turn it off, which check-host reports as their choice), the official relay runs the same repo unit at 950 Mbit/s on ens16, and check-host fails a relay host without it, coturn locked to the relay's own peers, the 64 MB payload ceiling and hard backpressure. G2 (0.13): every frame is charged in work units (frames, the writes they cause, signature checks, door proofs, audience size) against session buckets sized above a member of ten 1,536-device servers, and a frame the bucket cannot pay for closes the socket with code 1013 and a reconnect hint (`breaker.h`). A session's frame is never dropped: it stays uncounted and is resent on resume. A fetch or guest socket, which has no session, loses that frame and pauses 30 s. It shipped in shadow mode in 0.13: the relay counted what it would trip and closed nothing, `auth_ok` names the credit, and `enforced` is set only on an enforcing relay, so clients pace themselves the same way (`relay_pace.rs`) without waiting on a shadow relay. Since 0.14 it enforces by default: the official relay's shadow counters over 11 days, the G1 scale run and a probe of 300 fresh sessions behind one address showed no trip; `BREAKER_MODE=shadow` is the operator's way back, and the setup menu names the mode. The fresh-start floor follows the operator's connection rate and burst. The device ladder steps only when a session overspends the whole honest budget within 10 minutes (cap plus 10 minutes of refill, 1.4M units); any other trip is a pause. Separately, the relay's held bytes are bounded (`send_budget.h`): at most 64 MB per socket and a global budget of a quarter of RAM by default, never below 512 MiB or a third of a smaller box's RAM, which an operator may change but not push below that floor, charged per address share; under pressure the least progressed reader of the heaviest share is closed first. That is memory safety, not a volume quota. The breaker's numbers are compiled in; an operator cannot lower them. | Vitalii | When an honest client is reported tripping the breaker, or when the per-share scheduler (A10) is built. Done in 0.14: the breaker enforces (track t14-relay). The plan-level tests for junk lock proofs and non-member leaves (relay mutations C6 and C9) and the guest refusals ahead of the send budget landed in 0.14 (track w2-d, `security_write_gates` §52), and a guest's JSON `msg` broadcasts now share its binary broadcasts' ten a minute. |
| AR-03 | Inside a group call, any participant can forge media frames that appear to come from another participant (RFC 9605 7.2, shared SFrame keys). | Accepted (2026-09-26) | As far as we know, no mainstream E2EE call system (Signal, WhatsApp, Meet, Zoom) signs individual frames; all share symmetric frame keys among participants. The forger must be an admitted participant running a modified client. Per-frame signatures cost CPU on every phone. Outsiders cannot forge: the GCM tag stops them, and nonce uniqueness in shared-key mode was checked and found safe (lead L-01, phase E+F media review). | Vitalii | If a mainstream system ships per-frame sender authentication. |

| AR-05 | Any peer in a room we share can ask us for an asset by its hash (server emotes, stickers, GIFs, banners, avatar frames and animations, personal emotes), learn whether we hold it and receive its bytes (candidate H20). | Accepted (2026-09-27) | Assets are content-addressed: knowing a hash almost always means the asker has already seen the asset, since server emotes live in the server's state and profile media hashes ride the profile updates people already receive. Since 0.12 (design A) the requests and answers (`EmoteRequest`, `EmoteAssets`) ride inside Olm on the carried lane, so the relay sees neither. | Vitalii | If assets ever need access control beyond a shared room. |
| AR-06 | Every `FileRequest` re-encrypts and re-streams the whole file, so a peer entitled to a file can make us upload it repeatedly (candidate H21). | Accepted (2026-09-27) | Honest clients ask only when they need the bytes (a download, a decrypt-fail retry). Only an entitled requester is served: our own devices, the DM counterpart, a member who can read the channel, or anyone for a file in a public channel. The per-sender frame budget (20 frames a second per device) limits how often one device asks, not the work each ask costs: every ask re-reads and re-encrypts the whole file. Since 0.14 the relay fallback no longer queues all its chunks at once, for files, vault shards and recovery-pool shards alike: a stream sends at the socket's pace from a fixed 4 MiB budget per device, and an ask for a file or shard whose relay stream to the same device still runs starts nothing. A device back on a fresh session ends its streams at once, and one that left every room we share (lost, or only hidden and still receiving) keeps its stream until it asks again, when the ask replaces it, so a retry is always served. A device runs at most 16 relay streams per asking identity and 64 in all, with at most 272 MiB staged on disk (one stream alone may hold more). When full, the asker holding the most gives way, public-channel guests before members; our own sends and devices stand outside these caps. A dedup window that outlasts the stream would break the retry. Re-checked with G2 (2026-10-23): the requester pays for its request frame and the serving device pays for the re-stream; bytes are not counted, and volume fairness on the official relay is left to CAKE on its NIC. | Vitalii | The post-0.13 file-traffic measurement (direct, TURN and relay fallback for a large file in a large server). |
| AR-08 | Report counts per target on the relay can be inflated with throwaway accounts (one report per reporter, target and category until the relay box reboots, when dedup starts over and the counts stay) (candidate I12, report half). | Accepted (2026-09-27); the full-table half fixed in 0.14 (2026-10-31, track b14-bounds) | Nothing acts on the counts automatically; only the operator reads them. Any future automatic enforcement must not rest on raw counts. The full-table half is fixed in 0.14: the fingerprint table used to hold at most 500,000 reports and take no more once full, so a flood could stop later reports from counting until the box rebooted. Now each report is charged to the reporter's address share and the table is bounded by bytes (128 MiB). Once it is full, the share holding the most gives up its least recently used report and that report's count, so a flood from one address block pushes out only its own reports and every new report still counts. Every report weighs the same, so string length never decides which goes. Counts carried from a file whose fingerprints the key cannot read stand outside the budget and never give way to a flood. Shares ride the restart snapshot, never the disk. | Vitalii | Before any automatic enforcement reads the counts. |

| AR-11 | A demoted admin can still backdate ops to before its demotion, and every member admits them at that time (design E residual R2). Since 0.13 such a backdated role grant also buys MLS precedence while the owner is away (`node/standing.rs` ranks by role tier first). A backdated admission buys no position: an admittee never ranks before its admitter's current span, and one whose admitters all left ranks last within its role tier. Until 0.14 the same held for a kicked or banned member; since 0.14 a kick or ban voids only the target's backdated power-granting ops, so its other backdated acts still fold. | Reopened 2026-11-04 and narrowed in 0.14 for kicks and bans (decision "void granted powers only", track t14-cover): a kick or ban names, by 16-byte content hash, every power-granting op of its target it had seen in the span it closes (admissions, role changes, permission and channel grants, labels given to others, join lock moves, lifted bans; at most 1,024, the rest marked by clock) and the base that decides the rest; every member voids a target op of those kinds dated inside that span and named nowhere. Still accepted: (1) demotions (2026-09-27); (2) a kicked or banned member's backdated acts that grant nothing (kicks, bans, mutes and demotions of others, channel and label removals, renames) still fold inside the span the removal closes; (3) a leave carries no list, so a member that left, was admitted again and is then kicked can backdate grants into its older span, which its own leave closed (review 2026-11-04, MED-3); (4) once a checkpoint decided the removal (its op gone, only the span end recorded), every grant of the target dated in that span that arrives later is void, honest or not and from whatever version the removal came, so a 0.13 kick voids nothing only while its op is retained (the silent re-ask seats a voided admittee again); (5) a rebuild drops a voiding removal that is refused at its own point and folds again, at most four passes: a removal dropped in one pass that folds in a later one voids nothing, and a longer chain keeps the fourth pass. | A demotion closes no span, so nothing marks where the demoted admin's authority ended; without a total order handed out by an authority, no decentralized design tells a backdated op from one authored offline before the demotion (Matrix state resolution has the same gap). Such ops lose every register a later honest write touched, and the admin could do the same things while it held the role. The same holds for (2): every such act is one the member could make while it was a member, and later honest writes win. (3) needs a leave and a re-admission before the kick; giving a leave a list would make every leave a signed audit of grants for one narrow case (no new design rounds for narrow cases, 2026-11-04). (4) and (5) only ever void more or less of the target's own grants, never anyone else's acts. A kick or ban from before 0.14 carries no list and voids nothing while its op is retained. | Vitalii | Reviewed 2026-10-01 with ID-1 and 2026-10-23 with the 0.13 standing rule. 2026-11-04: kicks and bans closed (tests `rev3_cover_a_kicked_member_backdates_an_admission`, `a_ban_after_a_leave_voids_an_admission_backdated_into_that_span`, `a_member_kicked_and_admitted_again_cannot_backdate_into_its_old_span`); 2026-11-05: a refused removal voids nothing (`review4_a_kicked_admins_refused_removal_voids_honest_grants`, `review4_a_demoted_admins_refused_removal_voids_honest_grants`, `a_refused_removal_leaves_the_tail_and_a_rebuild_equal`, `a_removal_parked_by_a_late_kick_of_its_author_voids_nothing`). Next for demotions: if ops ever gain a verifiable author time. |
