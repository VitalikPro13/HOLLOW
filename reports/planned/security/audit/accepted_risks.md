# Accepted risks

What we knowingly do not defend against, why, and when we look again. An
entry here is a decision, not an oversight: each has an owner and a reason
that survives a professional auditor reading it.

| ID | Risk | Decision | Reason | Owner | Review |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AR-01 | The relay has no per-message rate limits, although ASVS 17.3.1 wants signalling to survive floods and RFC 9420 16.8 says KeyPackage requests SHOULD be rate-limited. | Accepted (2026-09-26) | Message and binary rate limits were tried and broke the core: a reconnect burst (sync requests, channel probes, profiles, signalling) exceeds any limit, and dropped frames wedge CRDT sync. The protections that stay: per-IP connection caps (34 per IP, 10 new per minute, IPv6 /64), guest mode with its own limits and room cap, CAKE per-host fair share on the NIC, coturn locked to the relay's own peers, the 64 MB payload ceiling and hard backpressure. Memory: `feedback_relay_rules`, `project_relay_ip_limits`, `project_relay_fairshare_turn_lock`. | Vitalik | When flood tests exist (phase F, WP4): measure what a single authenticated peer can make the relay or other clients do. |
| AR-02 | Every device holds the master key, so a stolen usable device can revoke its owner's real devices (they wipe themselves), issue destroy orders, and keep the identity. | CLOSED (2026-10-01, HOL-SEC-077): design ID-1 built. Was **not accepted: fixed by design ID-1** (2026-09-26) | The recovery phrase, never stored on any device, becomes the root of authority. See the plan's section "Identity authority design". Until ID-1 ships the whitepaper's "Stolen/lost device" row must not promise more than this. | Vitalik | When ID-1 ships (target 0.12). |
| AR-03 | Inside a group call, any participant can forge media frames that appear to come from another participant (RFC 9605 7.2, shared SFrame keys). | Accepted (2026-09-26) | As far as we know, no mainstream E2EE call system (Signal, WhatsApp, Meet, Zoom) signs individual frames; all share symmetric frame keys among participants. The forger must be an admitted participant running a modified client. Per-frame signatures cost CPU on every phone. Outsider protection (nonce uniqueness, lead L-01) is NOT covered by this entry and must be verified. | Vitalik | If a mainstream system ships per-frame sender authentication. |
| AR-04 | Traffic analysis, relay availability, post-quantum, trust on first use, a compromised unlocked running device. | Accepted | Listed in WP 23.2 and claims.md "What we do not promise". | Vitalik | At each whitepaper revision. |
| AR-05 | Any peer in a room we share can ask us for an asset by its hash (server emotes, stickers, GIFs, banners, avatar frames and animations, personal emotes), learn whether we hold it and receive its bytes (candidate H20). | Accepted (2026-09-27) | Assets are content-addressed: knowing a hash almost always means the asker has already seen the asset, since server emotes live in the server's state and profile media hashes ride the profile updates people already receive. The requests travel in plaintext, which the class A work moves into encrypted transport. | Vitalik | With the class A design. |
| AR-06 | Every `FileRequest` re-encrypts and re-streams the whole file, so a peer entitled to a file can make us upload it repeatedly (candidate H21). | Accepted (2026-09-27) | Honest clients ask only when they need the bytes (a download, a decrypt-fail retry). Only an entitled requester (the DM counterpart, or a member who can read the channel) is served, the per-sender frame budget bounds the rate, and a dedup window would break the retry. | Vitalik | With the relay traffic measurement in phase G. |
| AR-07 | Once the relay's offline buffer holds its global ceiling of 65,536 recipient keys, a sender holding a single key evicts the oldest deposit of anyone, so many throwaway accounts can push real parked messages out (candidate I9). | CLOSED (2026-09-29, HOL-SEC-070): every relay table a stranger can fill evicts by address share, so throwaway accounts on one address evict only each other. Was accepted (2026-09-27) | The buffer is an availability cache: an evicted message is not lost, the sender still holds it and sync delivers it once both sides are online. Filling it needs tens of thousands of accounts across many IP addresses (34 connections and 10 new per minute per IP). A refusing quota is the rule-breaking fix (`feedback_relay_rules`). | Vitalik | With the relay traffic measurement in phase G. |
| AR-08 | Report counts per target on the relay can be inflated with throwaway accounts (one report per reporter, target and category) (candidate I12, report half). | Accepted (2026-09-27) | Nothing acts on the counts automatically; only the operator reads them. Any future automatic enforcement must not rest on raw counts. | Vitalik | Before any automatic enforcement reads the counts. |
| AR-09 | A signed `KeyRequest` stays valid for the 300 s skew window, so the relay can replay a captured one (resetting a session after the 5 s cooldown), and any new identity can make us mint one-time keys, which can push a friend request's carried key bundle back to the live key exchange (candidate L6). | CLOSED (2026-09-27, HOL-SEC-054) | Was accepted because a replay rule broke honest session setup (identical same-second KeyRequests with deterministic signatures). Design A's sealed frames carry a random nonce, so honest duplicates differ, and the live-frame replay guard refuses a replayed KeyRequest. | Vitalik | Done. |
| AR-10 | A joiner of a server founded before 0.12 trusts the member that answers (trust on first use) when its invite carries no owner pin, or while the owner has not run 0.12 to sign the checkpoint (design E residual R1). Until that checkpoint, such a server's ops also apply in arrival order over a log capped at 1000, so a member or an Olm peer can hand on a genuine old op from outside that window, or hold one back, and change the order its replicas saw (phase B re-check row 0.5; added 2026-10-02, decision D2). A joiner pinned to such a server's owner takes the answering member's members, channels and settings (never its roles, bans or mutes, HOL-SEC-093) until a member serves the owner's checkpoint, so a hostile member that answers first can withhold the checkpoint for as long as no honest member answers (re-check crdt A-06). | Accepted (2026-09-27, extended 2026-10-02) | Those servers have no founding op anyone can prove: their history predates op signing and was capped at 1000. Every 0.12 server has a self-certifying id, every 0.12 invite to an older server pins its owner, and the owner's first 0.12 start signs the checkpoint. What stays is an old link, or an owner who never updates. | Vitalik | When 0.11 invite links have aged out. |
| AR-11 | A demoted or removed admin can still backdate ops to before its demotion, and every member admits them at that time (design E residual R2). | Accepted (2026-09-27) | Without a total order handed out by an authority, no decentralized design tells a backdated op from one authored offline before the demotion (Matrix state resolution has the same gap). Such ops lose every register a later honest write touched, and the admin could do the same things while it held the role. | Vitalik | Reviewed 2026-10-01 with ID-1: device co-signatures order device statements, never server ops, so this stays. Next: if ops ever gain a verifiable author time. |
| AR-12 | An op older than a checkpoint's `covers` (the newest clock the owner's state reflects) that the owner had not seen is dropped by every member (design E residual R3). | Accepted (2026-09-27) | Deterministic and rare: it takes an op dated before the owner's own newest one that never reached the owner, and the owner checkpoints once per existing server and then only when a log passes 2000 ops, at most hourly. Everything after `covers` survives, so an owner back from days away overwrites nothing that happened meanwhile. The author sees the change undone and can make it again. | Vitalik | If a lost change is ever reported. |
| AR-13 | A file sent before 0.12 has a random 32-character id that commits to nothing, so a holder answering a re-pull, or a member holding a channel file's key, can still hand over other bytes for it (HOL-SEC-060 residual). | Accepted (2026-09-28) | Only history is exposed: a 0.11 client cannot deliver anything to 0.12 at all, since 0.12 refuses unsealed frames. Its author signed only the id, and no one but the author can add a commitment afterwards. Every 0.12 file is committed, the length tells the two kinds apart so a new file cannot be passed off as old, and an old-style id a member mints on 0.12 only ever carries that member's own content. The old delivery gates (owner or asked holder, readable channel, bytes never replaced once on disk) still hold. | Vitalik | When pre-0.12 history has aged out of retention. |
| AR-14 | The app pins the first media forwarder each relay names and refuses another for 7 days while the pinned one stays online, so on the very first contact a relay that is already hostile can name a forwarder of its choosing (A-D4 residual). | Accepted (2026-09-29) | The forwarder only ever carries SFrame ciphertext; what it learns is viewers' addresses and who watches whose share. Whoever runs the relay already runs its forwarder and sees every address, so a relay hostile from the start gains nothing by naming another one, and the pin covers a relay that turns hostile later. A forwarder id built into the app would make every forwarder change an app update. | Vitalik | If forwarders are ever run by someone other than the relay operator. |
| AR-15 | Design ID-1's residuals: a legacy identity (from before 0.12, phrase not yet confirmed) still lets the master key admit a device; someone meeting the identity for the first time after a master-key holder published a forged recovery key pins the forged one; whoever learns the phrase is the identity (the newest recovery wins). ID-1R (2026-10-02, HOL-SEC-078) closed the inbox half and left: after the relay box reboots, the first roster shown for an identity sets its recovery key at that relay, so a master-key holder who shows a forged one first holds that inbox there until the next reboot; a member that floods its own base pushes out devices deeper than itself until the phrase is typed (HOL-SEC-079); a thief's own vouchees made before its removal stay until removed too (HOL-SEC-080). Meetings (session 33 follow-up, checked in code 2026-10-04): a meeting host who holds no roster for a participant judges that participant's devices by the master-signed certificate alone (`mls_authority::refused` refuses only where a held roster leaves the device out), so a device the participant removed stays seated in a running meeting, can knock on a new one and be admitted, and a master-key holder can mint a device and appear as the identity in any such meeting whose link and `key=` it has. | Accepted (2026-10-01, ID-1R part 2026-10-02, meetings 2026-10-04) | The legacy window closes at the first confirmation, which the app asks for at the first 0.12 start and keeps reminding about. A forged key only fools contacts who never met the identity before; everyone who did keeps the real one and the owner's devices see the conflict. Meetings are the same first-contact case: a host who never met the identity has nothing to tell its devices apart by, every knock is still admitted by hand, the link and its key are needed, and participants who hold the roster (friends, co-members) refuse that device's chat and voice inside the meeting (HOL-SEC-123). The fix, if meeting impersonation ever matters: the host asks the relay for the participant's current roster fold (ID-1R) before admitting, at the cost of a new relay query and showing a stranger the participant's device list. A phrase is a bearer secret by design, the same as every recovery phrase. Nothing in an identity's id commits to its recovery key, so a relay that lost its memory cannot tell a forged key from the real one; reboots are rare and planned, and a service restart keeps the registry. A member's flood can only do what it could do by removing devices, and a new base gives it no standing. | Vitalik | When 0.11 identities have aged out, and if identity ids ever change. |
| AR-16 | Design D1's residuals (HOL-SEC-091): whoever joins through an open invite is a member and sees the room; someone who already knows a device id can address it in a server's room and learn from a reply that it is there (in a server with a public channel, where members answer strangers); a removed member keeps seeing the room for up to the 60 s grace after the lock moves; a lock record evicted under the fair-share budget leaves its room open until a member puts the chain back; legacy (32-hex) servers and meetings stay open as before. | Accepted (2026-10-02) | Scope Vitalik agreed for D1: it stops scrapers that hold only a server id. The grace is what lets the op carrying the new door reach the members at all. A known device id is already a link across rooms. Eviction needs an attacker holding many address blocks (phase G). Legacy servers have no lock the relay can key by room, as in AR-10. | Vitalik | With the phase G relay measurement, and when 0.11 servers have aged out. |
| AR-17 | A guest previewing a server (its public channels, before joining) is shown the server's name, avatar, banner thumbnail and list of public channels by whichever member answers, and nothing ties them to the owner (D6, re-check A-CH14 and A-CH16). | Accepted (2026-10-02) | The preview is a first look before a join, and the join itself is anchored (the server id or the owner pin). A member can already show a stranger anything it likes outside the app. A closed channel never reaches the list from an honest member (HOL-SEC-094), and every post and author name a guest sees is signed (HOL-SEC-095). | Vitalik | If server previews ever carry the owner's signature. |
| AR-18 | Design D5's residuals (HOL-SEC-096): a destroy order from an identity whose recovery phrase was never typed on 0.12 has no key the relay can check, so it stays an opaque deposit under the HOL-SEC-029 eviction rules; proven slots are bounded list-wide by address share, so an attacker minting free identities from many address blocks can still evict one; the relay now reads a parked order and learns that an identity ordered some of its devices wiped. | Accepted (2026-10-03) | The legacy window is AR-15's and closes at the first phrase confirmation, which the app asks for at the first 0.12 start. Eviction takes a real address block per slot pushed out, and the genuine order sits alone in its own share, so it is the last to go (the phase G class, as AR-16). The relay already knows every device of each identity whose roster it holds and which device ids a deposit targets; reading the order adds only that it is genuine. | Vitalik | With the phase G relay measurement, and when 0.11 identities have aged out. |
| AR-19 | Decision D7's accepted residuals (phase B re-check). The Olm part (transport A-T18) is closed in session 33: each sending device's frames are read through a saved mark, the newest seal time we decrypted from it, written after its session by the node and the push fetch, and a decrypt failure on a frame sealed at or before that mark is dropped with no retire and no KeyRequest. What remains of it: a sender whose clock steps back (relay auth holds honest clocks within 60 s) waits for a real re-key until its seals pass the mark, and a frame sealed before one we read that we never read ourselves (a gap past the 40 kept message keys, or a crossed session's frame in flight) is dropped without the re-key's DM resync and returns with the next catch-up. The rest: slow mode judges a post dated older than its window by the post's own signed time, so a member can backdate a burst past slow mode (channel A-CH01, decision 2b); a removed device's own removals still count until the phrase settles it, so a thief and the owner can remove each other and the owner must type the phrase within the 3-day grace (identity A-02); a reboot of the relay box (not a service restart) loses the restart snapshot and with it the legacy device-list marks of HOL-SEC-031, so a revoked pre-0.12 sibling can read its mailbox again there until its identity re-shows; the path closes for good when `ACCEPT_DEVICE_LIST_INBOX_PROOF` is switched off after 0.12 (relay A-02c). | Accepted (2026-10-02) | The Olm remainder needs an honest sender's clock to step back, or a frame already lost to its gap; either way the DM catch-up brings the message back. Backdating moves only the poster's own posts, which still pass every membership and posting gate, and slow mode is courtesy, not authority. Mutual removal is the ID-1 design: the phrase is the last word, the 3-day grace exists for it, and the owner's devices show the removal at once. Reboots are rare and planned; a phrase-rooted identity is held by its roster (AR-15) rather than by these marks. | Vitalik | If slow mode ever becomes a moderation guarantee, and when 0.11 identities have aged out. |
| AR-20 | A vault manifest is unsigned and its content id names no author, so the first manifest a member receives for a content id wins: a server member who lands one before the creator's sets that receiver's shard hashes, and the receiver then refuses (and deletes) genuine copies of that one content (HOL-SEC-117 residual, re-check files A-V10). | Accepted (2026-10-03) | Availability of one vault file at one receiver, from a member of that server; the bytes it serves are still checked against the content id, so nothing false is ever shown. The real fix binds the vault content id into the file's own signed commitment, a protocol change (send path, a new card field, FFI and three Dart call sites) not worth it before 0.12. | Vitalik | In phase F WP7 (files and assets). |
| AR-21 | The update manifest carries no expiry and the app keeps no newest-seen floor, so whoever controls the update host can keep serving an old, validly signed manifest and hide a newer release (a TUF freeze), and a failed update check stays quiet; the desktop updater trusts the signed manifest's SHA-256 alone and never checks the OS code signature of what it downloaded (phase E+F distribution C-DIST-04, C-DIST-05). | Accepted (2026-10-04) | We run the update host ourselves, over HTTPS. The Ed25519 key that signs the manifest stays offline, so even a compromised host can only withhold an update, never install anything, and a downgrade is refused. A held-back release still shows on GitHub, the website and the news feed. A real expiry needs a regular re-signing with the offline key. Someone who installs a modified fork trusts whoever made it. | Vitalik | If the update host is ever run by someone else, or with the phase G close-out. |
| AR-22 | Every Android release is signed with a debug-named certificate from a keystore generated on the release machine, under the Android default password, so possession of that one file is the whole signing key (phase E+F distribution C-DIST-03). | Accepted (2026-10-04) | The key itself is unique to that machine (the SDK generates it), never in the repo, and backed up offline; nobody can sign an update that installs over Hollow without the file. Changing the key breaks every upgrade unless done through APK signature scheme v3 key rotation. | Vitalik | Before a Play Store or F-Droid listing, or at the next key rotation. |
| AR-23 | Ko-fi signs nothing, so the shop's webhook knows an order is genuine only by the artist's Ko-fi verification token inside the POST body (`shop/src/lib/server/kofi.js` `ingestKofiEvent`); whoever holds that artist's token can post orders with fresh message ids and receive codes for that artist's items, up to the per-order ceilings (phase E+F files C-FILES-05). | Accepted (2026-10-04) | The token lives in the artist's own Ko-fi settings, and the shop keeps only its HMAC, compared in constant time, per artist; a missing token refuses. A leaked token reaches only that artist's own listings, and a code still cannot carry a credential to anyone else: redemption blind-signs and every client checks it against the pinned root (C-32). Keeping the token private is the artist's part, the same as their Ko-fi password. | Vitalik | With a per-artist daily mint cap in 0.12.x, or if Ko-fi ever signs its webhooks. |
| AR-24 | The App Lock secret is protected by Argon2id alone, with no hardware-enforced attempt limit, so someone holding a copy of the data folder can guess a numeric PIN offline; since 0.12 a new PIN has at least six digits (about a million guesses), and an older shorter PIN keeps working until its owner changes it (phase E+F local C-LOCAL-04, lead L-07, claim C-36). | Accepted (2026-10-04) | A passphrase is always allowed and closes this. A copy of the data folder already takes the device or its backups (Android opts out of backups, iOS data is excluded since 0.12), and the identity's own password mode is the stronger protection for a seized disk. Hardware attempt limits differ on every platform and need device testing. | Vitalik | Hardware-backed attempt limits (Android Keystore, iOS Secure Enclave) in 0.12.x. |
| AR-25 | Server MLS groups have no post-compromise recovery for a member that never commits: no leaf updates itself and add-only commits carry no path, so a one-time copy of a member's MLS state keeps reading that server's traffic until that member's leaf is replaced (a repair, a rejoin or a removal commit it makes) (phase E+F mls C-MLS-07; RFC 9420 section 16.6, RFC 9750 sections 7 and 8.2.2). | Accepted (2026-10-04) | Taking that copy needs the member's unlocked database or malware on its device, which reads the same traffic live anyway. Scheduled self-updates are concurrent commits, so they need careful fork testing that does not fit before 0.12. WHITEPAPER 5.4 is reworded to say exactly this. | Vitalik | Periodic self-update commits in 0.12.x. |
| AR-26 | Push wakes sent through Google (FCM) and Apple (APNs) carry the sender's device id, and for channel wakes the server id, channel id and mention flag, in the clear, so Google and Apple can see who messages a phone and which servers it belongs to; UnifiedPush already encrypts the same block (phase E+F relay_push C-RP-05). | Accepted until 0.12.x (2026-10-04) | No message content, name or key rides a wake, and the ids are random identifiers, not phone numbers or accounts. Encrypting the block for FCM and APNs touches the push sidecar, Android, the iOS extension and Dart and needs phone testing, so it follows the release. The whitepaper and privacy policy are corrected now so they promise nothing more. | Vitalik | Encrypted FCM/APNs wake payloads in 0.12.x. |
| AR-27 | Any member of a server can make its MLS group move to a new epoch whenever it likes (a commit that only updates its own leaf is accepted), so a hostile member can make every member re-key, and every voice call rotate its SFrame key, over and over (phase E+F mls C-MLS-01 variant b). In a meeting without a waiting room the same holds for a seated guest that knocks again: since 0.12 the host replaces its leaf in a new commit each time (that is what lets a guest who left come back), and a link holder could already force an epoch per fresh device id. | Accepted until 0.12.x (2026-10-04) | It costs only work and a brief media hiccup; no one reads or forges anything, and the member is visible and can be kicked. A limit belongs on the receiving side (epochs per committer per minute), the same class as the relay flood rules of phase G. | Vitalik | A receiver-side commit rate rule in 0.12.x, with phase G. |
| AR-28 | Images from peers still reach Flutter's image decoder (Skia and its libwebp) without a pure-Rust check first in two places: asset-rail blobs (emotes, stickers, avatar frames, banners), which are content-addressed and so cannot be re-encoded without changing their id, and image attachments that auto-download; an attacker who holds a decoder bug and can send us an image could reach it without a tap (phase E+F files C-FILES-04; thumbnails ARE re-encoded in Rust since 0.12). | Accepted until 0.12.x (2026-10-04) | It needs a memory-safety bug in Skia's image path, which ships with each Flutter release we keep current. Asset blobs pass magic, size and canvas checks, attachments pass the size caps and come only from friends and co-members. A pure-Rust decode as a check (original bytes passed on unchanged) and fuzzing of the decoder are phase G work. | Vitalik | Phase G fuzzing of Skia's WebP path and a pure-Rust pre-check in 0.12.x. |
| AR-29 | A device removal reaches a contact only when that contact hears it: contacts who were offline at the time learn it on a later roster exchange, nothing makes a held roster expire, and nothing notices two contacts holding different rosters for one identity, so a relay that withholds the removal from a contact keeps the removed device receiving that contact's DMs (phase E+F identity C-IDENTITY-05, lead L-05; WhatsApp answers this with a 35-day device list expiry). | Accepted until 0.12.x (2026-10-04) | Withholding needs the relay itself (or a self-hosted one) to turn hostile against one contact, and the removed device must still connect; every contact that does hear the removal stops at once, and the owner's own devices show it. Roster freshness (a digest riding DM traffic so a contact asks for a newer roster, perhaps an expiry) is a protocol change that needs a relay change and fleet testing. | Vitalik | A roster digest on DM traffic, and an expiry decision, in 0.12.x. |
| AR-30 | An Olm session is saved after the message it encrypted has left, without waiting for the save, so a kill in that window restarts on an older copy of the sending chain and the next message reuses a message key; whoever holds both ciphertexts learns where their plaintexts agree (phase E+F olm C-OLM-09). | Accepted until 0.12.x (2026-10-04) | It needs the app killed in the moment between an encrypt and its save, leaks only partial equality between two messages, and never a key. The fix (each send waits, bounded, for its session save; pickles stored max-wins on a send counter) touches every send path, too much right before the release. | Vitalik | Acknowledged session saves in 0.12.x. |
| AR-31 | Blocking someone does not recall copies of messages we sent them that the relay had already parked; they are delivered when that person next connects (phase E+F olm, C-OLM-02 follow-up). | Accepted (2026-10-04) | They are messages we sent before the block, on purpose; the relay holds them for at most its retention (3 days by default) and the blocked person reads nothing we send afterwards. Recalling them would need a new signed withdraw frame at the relay. | Vitalik | If a recall frame is ever added for another reason. |
